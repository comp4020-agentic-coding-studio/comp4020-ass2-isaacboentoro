# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

Both assessments accept a 48-hour grace period with no penalty, no
questions asked — email [Marguerite](/people/marguerite-voss/) to let her
know you're using it, but you don't need a reason. Beyond 48 hours, standard
university extension rules apply; ask before the deadline, not after.

## Declaring tool assistance

This course is explicitly about which tools you trust to check your work,
so it would be strange to be squeamish about which tools helped you write
it. Both assessments ask you to declare any AI or automated-assistance use
in a short note at the top of your submission — what you used it for, and
what you didn't. Undeclared assistance is treated as an academic-integrity
matter; declared assistance is not penalised on its own, and is marked on
the same criteria as everything else: does the verification actually hold,
and can you explain it.

## Academic integrity, generally

The standard university policy applies to everything not covered above —
you're presenting your own analysis of your own tool runs, not someone
else's proof log with your name on it. If you're unsure whether something
crosses a line, ask before you submit.

## Getting help

Studio sessions and the course's discussion channel are the fastest route
for anything tool-specific — a Frama-C proof obligation that won't
discharge, a Checked C conversion that won't type-check. For anything about
marks, deadlines or extensions, email the [teaching team](/people/)
directly rather than posting it publicly.

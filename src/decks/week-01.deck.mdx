# Compile-Time Memory Safety in C

Twelve retrofits, one broken language

---

## C, 1972

- no bounds checks
- no lifetime tracking
- no runtime, on purpose

Fast, portable, and exactly as safe as the programmer writing it

---

## The bill comes due

- buffer overflow
- use-after-free
- double-free
- uninitialised read

Five bug classes, forever, in every C codebase that has ever shipped

---

{/* _class: quote */}

> "Trust the programmer" was a design decision made for 1970s hardware.
> The hardware moved on. The decision didn't.

---

## This course is not "C is bad"

It's: **what has anyone actually built** to keep C's speed and reach while
closing this gap — and what did each attempt have to give up to do it?

---

## The shape of the semester

- weeks 2–3: what compile-time checking can prove at all, and Rust as the
  target everyone else is measured against
- weeks 4–10: seven real retrofits, from Cyclone to academic prototypes
- week 11: one CVE, every tool, side by side
- week 12: what's actually shipping today
